h(t) · inspiral → merger → ringdown

I turn gravitational-wave and multi-messenger observations into fast, trustworthy measurements of dense nuclear matter and of how black-hole binaries form.

I am a physicist working at the meeting point of gravitational-wave astronomy, nuclear astrophysics and data science. My work builds machine-learning surrogates that make neutron-star physics fast enough to run on every detection, and Bayesian inference pipelines that keep the errors of those surrogates visible.

I am currently an ANRF Postdoctoral Fellow at the Chennai Mathematical Institute. Before that I was an Institute Postdoctoral Fellow at IIT Bombay, and I received my PhD in Physics from Washington State University in 2023. I am a member of the LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA Collaboration and represent the LIGO extreme-matter group in the LIGO-India Scientific Collaboration.

Research

Research interests

Dense-matter equation of state

A multi-messenger framework that joins chiral effective field theory and phenomenological nuclear priors to an agnostic core, constrained by GW170817, NICER and heavy pulsars.

arXiv:2306.04386

Machine learning for neutron stars

Deep-TOV, a physics-informed network that maps an equation of state to neutron-star mass and radius, now being integrated into LALSuite for low-latency use.

arXiv:2405.08163

Post-merger signals & next-generation detectors

Hierarchical Bayesian stacking of binary-neutron-star post-merger signals, and forecasts for LIGO-India and third-generation observatories.

arXiv:2505.21667

Eccentric binary black holes

Fast time–frequency methods that constrain orbital eccentricity, a key signature of how black-hole binaries form in dense stellar environments.

arXiv:2603.09150

Waveform modelling

Mode mixing in higher-multipole waveforms for non-spinning binary black holes, with faithfulness above 99.6%.

Phys. Rev. D 100, 024032

Current directions

Extending Deep-TOV to tidal deformability and finite-temperature matter; testing whether all neutron stars share one equation of state; detecting gravitational-wave memory with the LIGO-India network.

Teaching

Teaching

I teach physics as the craft of building a model, computing its consequences and testing it against data. I teach in English and Hindi.

Phenomenon first

Open with something students can see or measure, such as a chirp in real LIGO data, then build the equations.

Compute early

Short Python notebooks let students solve, perturb and break the equations they learn.

Visible reasoning

An estimate before every calculation and an uncertainty with every answer.

One mini-project per course

Reproduce a known result, change one assumption, explain what happens.
